How to compress a video on iPhone

Three ways to make a video file smaller on iPhone, and what each one costs in quality.

Checked 23 Nov 2026 · by Emil Svetlichnyy, developer of Zipvid

Short answerA video gets smaller when you lower its resolution, its frame rate or bitrate, or re-encode it with HEVC. Apple's own way is exporting at a lower resolution in iMovie; a compressor app such as Zipvid does it in one step on the iPhone. Any compression can lower visual quality, so check the result before you delete the original.

Steps

  1. Decide where the video is going (messenger, email, storage). That sets the size you need.
  2. Pick a method: iMovie export at lower resolution, a compressor app, or shoot smaller next time (Settings → Camera).
  3. Compress a copy, not the original. Keep the original until you have watched the result.
  4. Watch the smaller file on your iPhone and check sound, sharpness and length.
  5. Delete the original only if you are happy, and empty Recently Deleted if you want the space back now.

Apple's size estimates for one minute of video

Apple lists these rough iMovie export sizes for one minute of footage shot with default iPhone settings:

Export resolutionApprox. size per minute
1080p (default)70–105 MB
720p19–27 MB
540p12–16 MB
360p6–9 MB

Source: Apple Support, "Compress a video on iPhone". Your numbers depend on the clip.

FAQ

Can I compress a video without losing any quality?

Not completely. Apple notes that quality might be reduced when you lower the export resolution, and a smaller file means less data. A higher bitrate or HEVC keeps more detail for the same size, but it is still a trade-off.

Does compressing a video delete the original?

No, as long as you save a copy. Keep the original until you have checked the smaller file.

Is it safe to use a compressor app?

Check where processing happens. Zipvid compresses on the device and does not upload your videos.
